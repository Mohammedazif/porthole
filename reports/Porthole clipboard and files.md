# Stage USB files, the pasteboard, and a same-Wi-Fi app

On an iPhone 14 Pro running iOS 26.6.1, clipboard transfer and file sharing both ways are possible without a jailbreak, and neither one comes from the AirPlay mirror or the Bluetooth keyboard. With nothing installed, Porthole already types the PC clipboard into a focused field as US QWERTY keystrokes, and one USB Trust adds a copy of photos and videos that are actually stored on the phone plus documents of apps that already turn on file sharing. The first thing to build on this machine is that file drawer, in C, through libimobiledevice, with Developer Mode left off. The next USB slice, off by default, mounts Apple’s Developer Disk Image and talks to `com.apple.coredevice.pasteboardservice`, so plain text and one ordinary image move both ways between the real pasteboards while the cable is in; the owner still pastes on the phone, and the only published success is an iOS 26.2 phone after a current disk image, not this 26.6.1 phone. The path the owner asked for with the cable out is a personal iPhone app, built in Xcode: Blip’s same-Wi-Fi share, plus the pasteboard. A share sheet sends the files the owner picked into the Porthole window, and while that app is in front the two clipboards exchange plain text and one image so a normal Paste works on either side. iOS will not keep the app awake to watch copies made in other apps. Apple’s Windows clipboard promise is an EU accessory API aimed at fall 2027. It is not an implementation option in November 2026.

## Four layers cover both directions, and the app is the unplugged one

The build order is the permission order. iOS will hand a trusted computer the camera-roll media folder and the Documents directory of apps that opted into file sharing. It will hand the general pasteboard to a developer service inside a mounted disk image, and to an app that is actually in front. It will hand an arbitrary file to whatever the owner just tapped Share on. It will not hand any of those to a keyboard report or to an AirPlay receiver. LetsView and ApowerMirror, the commercial AirPlay receivers, document pixels, recording, and screenshots, not `UIPasteboard` ([LetsView Windows FAQ](https://letsview.com/faq/how-to-use-windows-letsview.html); [fossbytes, 2021-05-28](https://fossbytes.com/apowermirror-letsview-mirror-iphone-to-pc-using-these-tools/)). Porthole’s own paste path never asks the phone for anything: Left Alt+V calls `SDL_GetClipboardText` and emits key-downs ([src/polish/runtime.c](../src/polish/runtime.c); [src/control/paste_sdl.c](../src/control/paste_sdl.c)).

| Direction | Nothing installed | Slice 1, USB files | Slice 2, USB pasteboard | Same-Wi-Fi app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PC text into the focused field | Alt+V, ASCII, US layout | unchanged | unchanged; still the unplugged typing path | unchanged; the app fills the pasteboard instead of typing |
| PC text or one image onto the iOS pasteboard | no | no | yes, then the owner pastes on the phone | yes, while the Porthole app is in front, then the owner pastes |
| iPhone copy of text or one image onto the PC clipboard | no | no | yes, while the cable is in and the image is mounted | yes, while the app is in front, or after one Paste tap in the app |
| Photos and videos that are stored on the phone, iPhone to PC | USB Trust, then AFC or Windows Photos | a button in the Porthole window | unchanged | share sheet; USB DCIM stays the originals |
| Other files, both ways | only apps that already share Documents; or Files to an SMB share the owner drives by hand | the same Documents folders, as buttons | unchanged | share sheet into the window, and the app’s Documents the other way |

Read the table as a stop rule. Slice 1 does not grow into the pasteboard. Slice 2 does not grow into “any file, any time, no cable.” The app does not grow into a background watcher, and it does not replace the USB copy of camera-roll originals. None of this was run on the owner’s phone in the research pass. The plan below says where that test is the gate.

## Nothing installed already types ASCII and copies local photos

Keep Alt+V, and rename it in the interface to **“Type clipboard into the focused field.”** The handler accepts Left Alt only, copies into an **8192-byte** buffer, and **refuses the paste entirely when the text is 8192 bytes or longer**. The encoder then keeps at most **2048** key events, skips every byte at or above `0x80`, and maps only printable ASCII plus newline, tab, and backspace, with Shift as the only modifier ([src/polish/runtime.c](../src/polish/runtime.c); [src/control/paste.c](../src/control/paste.c); [src/control/paste.h](../src/control/paste.h)). “café” loses the é. Emoji disappear. A copied picture contributes nothing, because the call is the text API. The keystrokes land only in a focused editable field. They do not write `UIPasteboard`, so a later Paste inside an iPhone app still pastes whatever the phone already had. Secure fields, Photos, Files, and the Home Screen get nothing useful. The same limit holds if the HID transport is Bluetooth or USB: a keyboard usage page is keys, not a pasteboard write. PC Ctrl is already Command on the phone, which is why Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V stay inside iOS ([README.md](../README.md)). That fact matters in slice 2. It is not a clipboard channel today.

The other zero-install capability is the cable, after one trust. The first time the phone is plugged in, the owner unlocks it and taps Trust (Apple’s article also says to enter the passcode). A trusted computer can sync photos, videos, contacts, and other content, and it stays trusted until Reset Location & Privacy or an erase. On iOS 16 and later the alert also returns when backing up, and if automatic computer backups are on it returns on every connect ([Apple Support 109054](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109054)). The record is not a flag. The two sides exchange 2048-bit RSA keys, the host receives a 256-bit key that unlocks an escrow keybag, and protected services run only inside that SSL session. A pairing record unused for more than 30 days expires. A Wi-Fi session is allowed only after a previous USB or Thunderbolt pairing, and Apple’s description of that session does not include a second prompt ([Apple Platform Security, pairing model](https://support.apple.com/en-au/guide/security/secadb5b6434/1/web/1)). On Linux, usbmuxd stores the record under `/var/lib/lockdown` ([usbmuxd README](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/usbmuxd)). On Windows, Apple’s stack is Apple Mobile Device Service, the usbmux API on TCP port 27015, which is what an iTunes or Apple Devices install provides ([pymobiledevice3 protocol notes](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/blob/master/misc/understanding_idevice_protocol_layers.md)). The Ubuntu VM sees the phone only if the USB device is passed through to the guest. That is an environment constraint, not a library feature.

What that trust actually opens, with no Developer Mode and no disk image, is narrow and useful. `com.apple.afc` is the media chroot. Photos sit under `DCIM`. The old full-filesystem service, `com.apple.afc2`, was a jailbreak service and has not been the non-jailbreak surface since about iOS 11 ([libimobiledevice issue 1679](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/libimobiledevice/issues/1679); [ifuse README](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/ifuse/blob/master/README.md)). Beside that, `com.apple.mobile.house_arrest` vends the Documents directory of an app whose install record has `UIFileSharingEnabled`, which defaults to NO ([Apple, UIFileSharingEnabled](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/uifilesharingenabled); [libimobiledevice issue 244](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/libimobiledevice/issues/244)). Apple’s own Windows tools are the same split. Photo import is Apple Devices from the Microsoft Store, then Trust, then Microsoft Photos, and it is not a general disk. File Sharing in Apple Devices copies only to and from an app that supports it; if no app does, the Files section is absent ([Apple, transfer photos, 14 Sep 2026](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267); [Apple Devices file sharing](https://support.apple.com/en-sg/120402)). Developer disk images are a different gate. libimobiledevice attaches them to screenshots and `ideviceimagemounter`, not to reading DCIM ([libimobiledevice README](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/libimobiledevice)).

Two stock Wi-Fi gestures also exist and are not worth building first. Files → Connect to Server lets the owner copy both ways onto a file server, as Guest or as a registered user. Apple’s page does not print the letters SMB; Samba interoperability reports do, and a server locked to SMB 3.1.1 has failed where `server min protocol = SMB3_02` worked ([Apple, connect servers with Files](https://support.apple.com/en-au/guide/iphone/iph8d8f0f1a0/ios); [Ask Different](https://apple.stackexchange.com/questions/436703/how-to-solve-ios-files-app-failure-to-connect-to-smb-server-up-to-ios-18)). The owner can point Files at a share the PC already has. Porthole should not embed a server to duplicate that. AirDrop, as Apple ships it, targets a nearby iPhone, iPad, or Mac. Open receivers need AWDL, which ordinary Windows and Ubuntu hardware do not offer, and contacts-only mode wants Apple identity keys taken from a Mac ([Apple AirDrop guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-airdrop-to-send-items-to-nearby-devices-iphcd8b9f0af/ios); [OpenDrop README](https://github.com/seemoo-lab/opendrop/blob/master/README.md)).

Universal Clipboard is the feature that looks like the goal and is not. It copies text, images, photos, and videos between nearby Apple devices on the same Apple Account with Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and Handoff on. The device list is Mac, iPhone, iPad, and Apple Vision Pro. Copying entire files is a Mac-to-Mac extra. There is no Windows or Linux row ([Apple Support 102430, 18 Feb 2026](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102430); [Apple Support 108046, 17 Mar 2026](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108046)). Signing into iCloud on the PC does not join it. In June 2026 Apple told the EU DMA process it would build an iPhone-to-Windows pasteboard share for a paired accessory: a framework in the style of the iOS 26.5 accessory notification APIs, carried by the Accessory Transport Extension, with one consent per accessory through AccessorySetupKit, engineering targeted for fall 2027, then a developer beta, then a public release. The press quotes do not name an iOS version. Headlines that say “iOS 28” or “early 2028” are the outlets’ guess, and several of them describe the filing as EU-only ([9to5Mac, 3 Aug 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/03/apple-plans-to-open-iphone-clipboard-access-to-windows-pcs/); [The Verge, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.theverge.com/tech/975020/apple-windows-pc-copy-paste-eu)). The interoperability portal page itself was not opened for this report. The quoted plan is not a silent LAN sync, it does not cover Linux, and it is not shipping.

What stays impossible if the owner refuses both Developer Mode and any Share tap is the right-hand side of the phone’s pasteboard, an image placed onto that pasteboard, and any file that is neither in the AFC media tree nor in a file-sharing app’s Documents. A mirrored screenshot is a picture of pixels. A copied photo that is not on screen is invisible to it.

## Slice one is a USB file drawer with Developer Mode left off

Build a small GPL C helper linked to libimobiledevice, started the way Porthole already starts a helper, and keep the buttons in the existing window. libimobiledevice speaks the native protocols, does not require a jailbreak, is LGPL-2.1, and runs on Linux and Windows. Its tree was moving on 18 November 2026 ([libimobiledevice README](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/libimobiledevice)). The helper’s command set is pair-validate, AFC list/get/put, and house-arrest `VendDocuments` for bundle ids whose install record has `UIFileSharingEnabled`. `installation_proxy` is the census that produces that list. It does not itself move bytes. `VendContainer`, the rest of an app sandbox, is implemented in the libraries and is not the surface Apple or iMazing document for a non-jailbroken phone ([house_arrest.py](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/blob/master/pymobiledevice3/services/house_arrest.py); [iMazing, app files](https://imazing.com/guides/how-to-access-your-iphone-apps-files-and-data)). There is no `--network` flag. Do not shell out to ifuse on Windows. ifuse is a Linux FUSE mount of the same chroot, and its documents mount has failed on at least one older package even when `--list-apps` worked ([ifuse issue 118](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/ifuse/issues/118)). Do not call `backup2`. That service can pull messages, WhatsApp, contacts, and call history. It is a different and broader copy than this feature ([pymobiledevice3 CLI recipes](https://doronz88.github.io/pymobiledevice3/latest/guides/cli-recipes/)).

The owner’s once-only setup for this slice is the trust already described, plus, on Windows, installing Apple Devices or iTunes so port 27015 exists, then replugging. On the Ubuntu dev VM it is usbmuxd plus USB passthrough. Whether Ubuntu 24.04’s apt package is new enough for iOS 26 was not checked. If pair or AFC fails on a current phone while upstream succeeds, build upstream. The helper detects a missing usbmux listener and says so. It does not bundle a driver. Whether a stock WinUSB device plus upstream usbmuxd is enough on Windows without Apple’s service was not proven, so the Windows setup text names Apple Devices.

Every day the owner plugs in and presses a button. One button copies photos and videos into a chosen PC folder. Another copies chosen PC files into the Documents folder of a file-sharing app the helper lists. If the list is empty, the window says that iOS only allows this for apps that turn file sharing on, and it does not invent a drop box. The built-in Files app has not been shown to expose such a container. Assume it does not until a listing on this phone says otherwise. Show the paired device name and refuse a different UDID. If Trust is missing, stop. If the trust alert is returning on every connect, say that automatic computer backups do that, and do not “fix” it by turning on Wi-Fi sync. A Linux report of AFC sessions dying while the phone still charges was not reproduced on iOS 26.6.0 by one commenter, and a crashed AFC service has historically stayed dead until replug ([libimobiledevice issue 1723](https://github.com/libimobiledevice/libimobiledevice/issues/1723)). Test that on this phone. Do not reject AFC because of it.

Originals mean the bytes in `DCIM`, not a share-sheet JPEG and not a screenshot of the mirror. Apple’s Windows import article says that if iCloud Photos is on, full-resolution originals have to be downloaded to the phone before a PC import. Optimize Storage defaults on: the full files live in iCloud, and the phone may keep a space-saving copy ([Apple, transfer photos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267); [Apple, photo and video storage, 1 Oct 2026](https://support.apple.com/en-au/105061)). The helper cannot reconstruct a file the phone has evicted. Albums, Recents, and Hidden are database views, not directories. A DCIM listing looks like Apple’s camera buckets, not the owner’s albums. A Live Photo on disk is a still plus a movie. Copy both, and do not treat an `.AAE` sidecar as the photo. The exact sibling names on iOS 26.6.1 were not listed. Edited assets were not tested. Camera → Formats → Most Compatible changes future captures to JPEG and H.264. It is not a converter for files already in the library, and the helper must not transcode on the way out. PTP, the other photo surface, is what Windows Photos uses, and it is the wrong library for Porthole. On an iPhone 16,2 running iOS 26.5, libgphoto2 saw an empty DCIM layout while the same phone browsed fine over AFC ([libgphoto2 issue 1254](https://github.com/gphoto/libgphoto2/issues/1254)).

The other direction has a hard stop. No Apple document found here says that dropping a file into `DCIM` makes it appear in Photos. Apple’s supported pushes are File Sharing into an app, or a photo-library sync, which is a different feature and was not re-read for deletion rules. Slice 1 therefore promises “this file is in that app’s Documents,” and, where the app also sets `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace`, “it shows up under On My iPhone in Files” ([Apple File Provider](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/fileprovider); [Finder file sharing, updated 6 Oct 2026](https://support.apple.com/en-us/119585)). It does not promise “this JPEG is now in Photos.” The button is an explicit action. A “new since last copy” filter is fine. A background pass that duplicates the camera roll whenever the cable appears is not.

Leave the pairing record where usbmuxd or Apple already put it. Do not copy `HostPrivateKey` or the escrow bag into the Porthole config, a screenshot, or a synced folder. Do not call `lockdown wifi-connections on`, and do not tick “show this iPhone when on Wi-Fi.” `afcclient --network` is documented as not recommended because the connection can die at any time ([afcclient man page](https://www.mankier.com/1/afcclient)). The mirror stays on AirPlay. The cable is an added channel, not a second screen. Whether a USB video-capture session can share that cable with AFC was not tested and is not part of this slice.

## Slice two opts into the real pasteboard after one disk image

Turn this on only from a setting that quotes Apple’s warning. Developer Mode “reduces the security of your device” because it opens developer-only functionality. It does not change App Store or TestFlight installs, and it is not a jailbreak. The owner turns it on under Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode, reads the alert, restarts, confirms, and enters the passcode. Turning it off also restarts the phone. The CLI that enables it without that UI works only when no passcode is set, so it does not apply to this phone ([Apple, enabling Developer Mode](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/enabling-developer-mode-on-a-device); [pymobiledevice3 CLI recipes](https://doronz88.github.io/pymobiledevice3/latest/guides/cli-recipes/)).

The service is not part of the base system. `PasteboardService` talks to `com.apple.coredevice.pasteboardservice`, served by `dtpasteboardd` from the mounted Developer Disk Image, over the iOS 17+ RemoteServiceDiscovery tunnel. On iOS 17.4 and later, including iOS 26, pymobiledevice3 builds that tunnel in-process with no root on Linux and on Windows ([pasteboard_service.py](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/blob/master/pymobiledevice3/remote/core_device/pasteboard_service.py); [iOS 17+ tunnels](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/blob/master/docs/guides/ios17-tunnels.md)). Image choice is versioned. iOS 17.0 through 26.x uses a PersonalizedDMG via `mounter auto-mount`, which downloads the image and does not need a Mac with Xcode. The cache is `~/.pymobiledevice3` or `$XDG_DATA_HOME/pymobiledevice3`, and it stays out of the repo. **The mount is required again after every reboot.** An old image is the failure mode that looks like “iOS 26 has no pasteboard”: on an iPhone 13 running iOS 26.2, and on an iPhone SE running iOS 17.5.1, the service was absent while a 17A PersonalizedDMG was mounted, other CoreDevice services still worked, and mounting build 27A5194q made the service appear. Copy and paste then “worked perfectly, including Chinese text and emoji.” The host in that report was macOS. Images were not mentioned ([pymobiledevice3 issue 1744](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/issues/1744)). No source states that the November 2026 image for iOS 26.6.1 contains the daemon, or that the build manifest lists the iPhone 14 Pro. The cryptex path is for iOS 27 and for a 26.4+ install that `mounter auto-mount` is not using on 26.x. The devices called out as missing from a PersonalizedDMG manifest are the iPhone 18 series, which ship with iOS 27, not the 14 Pro.

The gate is a spike, not a UI. On this phone, after Trust and Developer Mode, run `mounter auto-mount`, confirm `pasteboardservice` is in the RSD list, then round-trip ASCII, a non-ASCII string, and a PNG with `read_pasteboard` / `set_text` / `set_image`. If the service is missing, run auto-mount again before calling it a dead end. That spike has not been done. A Windows or Linux success report was not found. The library is cross-platform. The detailed bug was not.

If the spike passes, the helper is a separate GPL Python process, pinned to a pymobiledevice3 release that contains the September 2026 stall fix. **11.15.2** is the floor. **11.59.0** was the PyPI release on 18 November 2026 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pymobiledevice3/); [v11.15.2](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/releases/tag/v11.15.2)). Porthole is already GPL-3.0. The maintainers say there will be no commercial license exception, and releasing the caller as GPL is the allowed path ([pymobiledevice3 docs](https://doronz88.github.io/pymobiledevice3/latest/)). Do not reimplement RemoteXPC or the type policy. Those bugs were still being fixed in September 2026.

Behavior, both ways, is one text value and at most one image. Text is tried as `public.utf8-plain-text`, then `public.plain-text`, then `public.text`. Images, lossless first, are PNG, JPEG, HEIC, TIFF, GIF, and WebP. `set_image` defaults to `public.png`. The monitor watches the Darwin notification `com.apple.pasteboard.notify.changed` and then pulls the primary type. It does not poll, and it does not use the daemon’s own change push, because that push makes `dtpasteboardd` load every representation first. A Notes copy advertises `public.rtf`, which fails only after about 64 seconds, and Apple’s own `devicectl device pasteboard monitor` showed the same stall. A picture that exists only inside a Notes web archive is skipped unless `allow_full_pull` is set. Leave that flag false, and tell the owner to copy pictures from Photos or from a screenshot ([PR 1961](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/pull/1961); [screen-mirroring guide](https://github.com/doronz88/pymobiledevice3/blob/master/docs/guides/screen-mirroring.md)). No byte ceiling was found. Video UTIs are not in the image list, so a copied video is not this slice. Rich text is flattened. What was already on the pasteboard when monitoring starts is not reported as a change, so the window needs an explicit “pull now” or the first copy after connect will look like a failure.

Phone to PC: a new plain-text copy becomes the PC clipboard, and a plain image becomes one image on the PC clipboard. The project’s browser path converts a device JPEG to PNG before handing it to the browser. A native Windows write should do that same conversion rather than assume the OS clipboard accepts HEIC. That format choice is the library’s browser behavior, not an Apple guarantee. PC to phone: text and one image copied on the PC are written with `set_text` / `set_image`. The status line says **“On the iPhone, paste with Ctrl+V.”** Writing the pasteboard does not focus a field and does not press Paste. Alt+V remains the path that types immediately, including when this slice is off or the cable is out. The two features answer different gestures. Conflating them is how the current hotkey got mislabeled.

Do not use `serve-web` or `serve-vnc` as this feature. Both refuse to start before iOS 27, because older systems do not send that screen stream. `serve-vnc` clipboard is text only and the server-side decode is macOS-only. Binding either to `0.0.0.0` means anyone who reaches the port can watch and control the phone ([CLI recipes, screen streaming](https://doronz88.github.io/pymobiledevice3/latest/guides/cli-recipes/)). The helper stays on USB. It does not turn on wireless debugging. After a phone reboot, or after an iOS update whose image is stale, auto-mount runs again before clipboard calls are expected to work. Developer Mode stays off unless this setting is on, and the setting says the owner can turn the switch off later at the cost of losing pasteboard sync.

## The same-Wi-Fi app does Blip’s file share and also the pasteboard

This is the slice the owner asked to add. It is a personal app on their own iPhone, used the way Blip uses the local network, and it does one thing Blip’s share does not: a copy on one side is a paste on the other. Blip signs in with email, lists online devices, and can send through an internet relay. Porthole keeps only the local piece. The phone and the PC are on the same Wi-Fi. After one pair, this phone is accepted with no prompt. Files the owner shares land in the Porthole window. There is no account, no directory of other people, and no relay.

“Direct” means the real pasteboards, not more keystrokes. While the Porthole app is the front app, a copy on the PC is written onto `UIPasteboard`, and a copy on the iPhone is written onto the PC clipboard. Text keeps the characters Alt+V drops, including “café” and emoji. One image moves as PNG. The owner then pastes with the phone’s own Paste. On this keyboard that is Ctrl+V, because Ctrl is already Command. The PC side is the normal PC paste. Neither side types into a field. Alt+V stays for the case where a field is already focused and the app is not open. A write onto the pasteboard does not focus a field and does not press Paste. The status line on the PC says **“On the iPhone, paste with Ctrl+V.”**

iOS will not make that silent. The phone will not host a standing server. KDE Connect’s iOS app says iOS has no supported way to keep a daemon-style process running, and being unresponsive in the background is intended ([kdeconnect-ios README](https://github.com/KDE/kdeconnect-ios/blob/master/README.md)). Nothing reviewed subscribes to “the user copied something” while the companion is suspended. Since iOS 16, a direct read of another app’s copy raises a prompt unless the person uses the system Paste command, taps `UIPasteControl` inside Porthole, or sets Settings → Apps → Porthole → Paste from Other Apps to Allow. There is no API to query Allow or Deny. A denied read looks like an empty pasteboard ([UIPasteControl](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uipastecontrol); [OWASP MASTG-KNOW-0083](https://mas.owasp.org/MASTG-KNOW-0083)). Shortcuts can Get Clipboard, and that action is explicitly unsupported in the background, and no automation found here fires on copy ([ScPL, getclipboard](https://docs.scpl.dev/actions/getclipboard)). A custom keyboard that stays on screen inside other apps is a later experiment. One commercial clipboard keyboard describes that pattern. This research did not open Apple’s keyboard-extension rules, so the plan does not promise it.

In the mirror, the gesture is short. The owner is looking at the phone through the window. With the cable out, they open Porthole on the phone, which is one tap in the mirror. The two sides exchange the current clip. They go back to the app they were in and paste. If Paste from Other Apps is Allow, that open is silent. If it is Ask, the app shows a Paste button built on `UIPasteControl`, and the system hands over the clip without the alert. A Share from Photos or Files does not need that button. The system passes the item the owner picked, which is why the share sheet never raises the paste prompt.

Dragging the photo that is on screen off the Porthole window takes that file. The press has to start on the picture. Movement that stays on the picture still controls the phone. Once the pointer has left the picture by 64 pixels, Porthole releases the phone button and offers a file to the desktop. The PC listens on TCP 47731. The code is in F1 help and in the log. The iPhone app, `ios/PortholePull`, indexes the photo library with the same difference hash, and while that app is open it returns the one matching original. Two close photos are not a guess: the drag keeps the screen picture and the status says so. A drag with the app closed also keeps the screen picture, saved under the Porthole config directory in `pull/screen.png`. The cable copy of `DCIM` remains the way to take every original, including the ones iCloud has not kept on the phone.

Files go both ways on the LAN, and originals still prefer the cable. The share extension asks for a file representation, not a public JPEG, so a HEIC and a Live Photo movie are what the owner picked when Photos provides them. A large Photos selection can still drop files, and a browser-style picker commonly turns HEIC into JPEG before the receiver sees it ([localsend issue 2653](https://github.com/localsend/localsend/issues/2653); [DEV Community, 10 Nov 2026](https://dev.to/heicnow/handling-heic-uploads-from-iphones-in-2026-what-breaks-and-how-to-fix-it-58a2)). That is why slice 1’s `DCIM` copy stays the originals path. The other direction drops a file on the Porthole window. It lands in the app’s Documents folder. `UIFileSharingEnabled` and `LSSupportsOpeningDocumentsInPlace` make that folder visible under On My iPhone in Files, and slice 1 can read the same folder over USB. The app does not promise that the file appears in the Photos library. Saving an unsupported type such as WebM into the photo library is a known failure in other LAN tools, and this app will not attempt that import ([localsend issue 2687](https://github.com/localsend/localsend/issues/2687)).

The channel is the PC listening, not the phone. Discovery is local mDNS only. The socket is TLS, and the app pins the PC key it saw at pairing. An iOS client aimed at plain HTTP or at a certificate it does not trust is the failure LocalSend already hit: on iOS 18.5 with LocalSend 1.17.0, iPhone sends failed while the other direction worked ([localsend issue 1268](https://github.com/localsend/localsend/issues/1268)). The first connection shows a short code on both sides. After the owner accepts, that phone is stored and later sends are not prompted. A different phone is refused. The listener binds to this machine’s LAN address. It does not listen for the public internet and it does not relay. LAN pairing is not USB trust and does not reuse the lockdown record. Clipboard messages are one UTF-8 string and at most one PNG. SDL2 only reads and writes clipboard text (`SDL_GetClipboardText` in [src/control/paste_sdl.c](../src/control/paste_sdl.c)), so the image goes through the platform clipboard. No Apple page found here states a pasteboard byte cap. Porthole sets its own cap rather than inventing one. File messages carry the name, the size, and the bytes, and the window shows the folder they landed in.

The app is built in Xcode on a Mac. This Ubuntu VM cannot sign it. A personal development signature needs Developer Mode, a restart, and a one-time trust of that developer certificate. Local Network permission is a separate prompt the product cannot remove. The free-account re-sign window was not verified against an Apple document, so the plan does not assume a signature stays valid. The mirror, the mouse, and Alt+V keep working with nothing installed ([README.md](../README.md)). The PC receiver can be written here before the phone app exists. The phone half waits for the Mac.

LocalSend and a hand-built Shortcut stay available and are not this product. LocalSend is Apache-2.0, has no account, and stays on the local network, with discovery on UDP `224.0.0.167:53317` and a receiver on TCP 53317 ([LocalSend](https://github.com/localsend/localsend); [localsend/protocol](https://github.com/localsend/protocol)). Each of its sends is accepted. It does not write this window’s paired inbox, and it does not sync the pasteboard. A Shortcut the owner runs can Get Clipboard or Copy to Clipboard, and iOS 27’s shortcut notes do not add an unattended clipboard sync. This phone is on iOS 26 ([9to5Mac, 29 Sep 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/09/29/ios-27s-shortcuts-app-adds-35-new-improved-actions-heres-whats-new/)). A 2023 shortcut that posts to bare `http://` is the version not to copy ([RoutineHub 17314](https://routinehub.co/shortcut/17314/)).

## Several designs stay unbuilt

The designs that stay unbuilt are the ones that pretend one of those prompts is optional. No jailbreak, no `afc2`, no SSH file server. No parser that tries to read the pasteboard out of AirPlay or BLE HID. No `serve-web` clipboard. No Wi-Fi lockdown and no wireless debugging as a silent retry when USB fails. No internet relay, no email account, and no sending to other people. No from-scratch RemoteXPC client. No use of a backup to sneak out the photo library or chat attachments. No PCAP and no syslog in this feature. No claim that dropping a file in `DCIM` fills Photos, until a test on this phone shows that it does. No background clipboard watcher inside the app, and no promise that a copy in Photos is seen while Porthole is suspended. Snapdrop and PairDrop need nothing installed and still fail the job: both ends sit in a browser, there is no folder, and the phone’s picker is the transcoding path. KDE Connect does not read other apps’ notifications and was not a clipboard bridge in the sources reviewed.

## Conclusion

The boundary on this phone is not “Windows cannot see iOS.” It is which human act the owner has already performed. Trust opens the camera-roll files. Developer Mode plus a mounted disk image opens the pasteboard with the app closed, and only while the cable is in. Opening the Porthole app, or tapping Share, is what moves files and the pasteboard on Wi-Fi. AirPlay and the keyboard sit outside all of those, which is why extending Alt+V cannot become images, files, or a read of the phone. The 2027 accessory API, even if it leaves the EU and even if it covers images, still does not move a camera roll or an arbitrary file. Slice 1 is the originals path and the part this machine can build with no Mac. Slice 2 is a developer session that has to be remounted after every boot, and it answers clipboard sync when the app is not open. The app is how copy and paste work when the phone is on the same Wi-Fi and the cable is out. It is a foreground exchange onto the real pasteboards, plus a share sheet. It is not a daemon, and it is not Blip’s relay.
